## 2. Information architecture and screens

**Competitor survey — from prior knowledge, UNVERIFIED current features/prices.** Apollo is associated with contact discovery/enrichment, targeting and sequences; Instantly with mailbox operations and campaign sending; Lemlist with personalised multistep outreach; HubSpot Sales with activity timelines, CRM pipeline and tasks; Pipedrive with clear deal stages and next actions. These are design references, not verified feature comparisons or marketing claims.

**Match:** filtered prospect lists, campaign sequences, reply inbox, activity timelines, pipeline and measurable outcomes. **Aim to improve:** one Aria can explain evidence, request approval and carry context into the wider business; a factual answer before a meeting request; visible controls and a human handover after one response. These are intended advantages, not demonstrated superiority. **Leave out initially:** a purchased contact database, automated LinkedIn activity, multichannel SMS/calls, mailbox fleets, synthetic warm-up networks, deliverability guarantees, tracking pixels, elaborate deal forecasting and unlimited AI conversations.

Add **Outreach** to the NS-B7 department menu. Routes proposed under `/app/outreach`; retain the business switcher, persistent Aria dock and contextual record IDs. Each screen starts with a short grounded Aria strip, last refresh time and “Ask Aria”. On mobile, lists become cards, filters a sheet, timeline/inbox panes separate views, approval actions reachable without horizontal scrolling. Keyboard labels, visible focus, non-colour status text, loading/empty/error states and retained filters are acceptance requirements.

Text enums have CHECK constraints; JSON has a schema version and bounded validated shape. Money/cost fields use decimal, never floats. Required fields are non-null unless called optional. Parent/child links use `(business_id,parent_id)` composite FKs wherever possible; links to existing tables without a matching unique key require a reviewed schema extension, not an unscoped lookup. No customer-provided business_id controls a write.

## 4. Research: decision makers and evidence

Pipeline: owner approves target criteria → deduplicate and check suppression → Companies House company identity/status/officers → permitted company website About/Team/contact pages → licensed search previews → evidence-backed role/fit assessment → proposed draft. A director is a possible decision maker, not proof of purchasing authority; prefer a relevant operational role supported by a dated company source. Missing contact details stay missing. Never fabricate an email or claim an address is verified because it has plausible syntax.

**Sources and boundary:** Companies House API is the intended free company/officer source per Mike's brief; current rate limits, terms and availability are **UNVERIFIED**. Store company number/status/type and only necessary officer name/role; do not retain DOB/home addresses. Company registration alone does not prove the mailbox subscriber or consent. Company-site fetches require permission/terms review, robots compliance, tight page/size/time budgets and redirect/domain checks. Reject private IPs, cloud metadata, credentialed pages, logins and paywalls; treat source text as untrusted data, never instructions to tools.

**Search recommendation:** Brave Search API, subject to verifying current licence permits the proposed search/preview storage and data processing. Allow only snippets delivered by the licensed search API and record source URL/query/time. A preview may reference LinkedIn; **never fetch, crawl, render, log in to, automate, use cookies against, or buy scraped copies of LinkedIn pages**. No profile enrichment endpoint/proxy that effectively scrapes LinkedIn. Do not follow a LinkedIn result URL in the research fetcher; manually viewing a source is an owner's separate action, not an automated instruction. If search terms prohibit snippet use, omit it. Current Brave pricing/terms are **UNVERIFIED**; the budget below is an assumed ceiling, not a quote.

**Fit proposal:** sector/service need 0–30, geography 0–20, organisation suitability 0–20, role relevance 0–20, dated trigger 0–10. Each component cites evidence; absent evidence scores zero with “unknown”, not a negative assertion. Display confidence separately. “Why now” must cite a dated expansion, hiring, service change or similar relevant public event; otherwise say “No timely trigger found”. Corporate eligibility and suppression are hard gates independent of score. Stale/officer-mismatch cases require review.

**Models:** configurable research, extraction, drafting and classification task profiles through a shared provider router; latest capable approved model chosen at implementation after current catalogue, DPA and eval checks. Exact current model/provider choice is **UNVERIFIED** offline. No new direct provider clients. If the router remains absent, a prerequisite ticket supplies it; no hidden call-site debt. Use bounded inputs, token/time limits, cost ceiling, recorded model/config version, approved fallback and no silent downgrade below eval quality. Model output cannot grant eligibility or permission. Evals: actual decision-maker precision, unsupported claims, source freshness, UK plain language, prompt injection, tenant isolation and answer-to-knowledge fidelity.

**Illustrative cost per completed prospect (assumptions, all UNVERIFIED prices):** 3 searches × $0.005 = $0.015; 12,000 model input tokens × $3/million = $0.036; 2,000 output × $15/million = $0.030; fetch/compute reserve $0.009; base $0.09, plus 30% retry/variance reserve = **$0.117**. At an assumed $1.25/£, about **£0.094**, or **£18.72 per 200**. This excludes reply processing, mailbox subscription, support and taxes; model upgrades may cost materially more. Budget ceiling proposed £0.20 per research attempt, daily tenant budget and provider-wide circuit breaker; pause before exceeding them. Price refresh and measured pilot economics precede paid launch.

Meters: `outreach_prospects_researched` increments once per completed evidence bundle, including a completed no-fit result; repeated delivery/retry of the same job is not another unit. Explicit refresh is another research job shown to owner. Failed attempts consume internal cost budget but no customer completed-prospect unit. Track searches, input/output tokens, costs and failures internally. `outreach_emails_sent` counts each accepted initial, follow-up or bespoke email once; ambiguous acceptance reserves allowance until reconciled. Reserve before work atomically; release definite failures. Never decrement suppression/objection events behind billing gates.

## 5. Sending, sequences and deliverability

Defaults proposed: one mailbox per campaign; first **five eligible recipients** individually reviewed as version-bound cards. Campaigns smaller than five review all. The card shows sender mailbox, To/CC, subject, body, AI sign-off, source/privacy and unsubscribe footer, permitted send window and estimated usage. Approval queues that exact version; editing any recipient/content/template/knowledge/sender invalidates the card. An approval is not authorisation for other prospects or future steps.

Manual approval remains default for every message until the owner explicitly enables the shared AUTO standing permission for that campaign/action type after the first batch. Permission covers approved template plus whitelisted factual variables, fixed target/brand/mailbox and limits; freeform bespoke wording stays a card. Revocation, expired permission, pause-all, new template/target, or lost entitlement cancels unsent authority. Display who authorised it, the version and what ran. Sending cannot be undone; “Pause” prevents future dispatch, never claims to recall mail.

**Defaults:** weekdays 09:00–17:00 Europe/London, DST-aware; recipients with unknown timezone use UK campaign scope only. Max 20 outbound outreach messages/day/mailbox initially, including all campaigns and bespoke replies; warm-up starts at 5/day for five sending days, then 10/day for five, then 20 only after owner review of bounce/reply health. No synthetic opens/replies or mailbox rotation. Tenant cap also 20 by default; lower remaining allowance/provider limit wins. Initial + two follow-ups, after 5 then 7 business days from actual accepted prior send; at most one sequence contact per prospect in seven calendar days, so schedule takes the later bound. No catch-up burst after downtime. Owner can choose lower caps; raising limits beyond pilot ceilings needs explicit settings review.

**Dispatch contract:** select due job → scoped fresh mailbox sync → transaction locks recipient/enrolment and quota → recheck suppression, any reply, takeover, entitlement, approval/permission version, mailbox ownership, freshness/domain health, time window and remaining caps → reserve usage and durably claim action → dispatch once → save provider IDs and acceptance → commit usage. Sync failure or stale cursor blocks sends. Concurrent workers use durable leases/unique job keys and recipient-level ordering; do not rely solely on a process flag. Crash/timeout after claim is unknown, never automatically retried. Provider reconciliation or owner-reviewed proof of non-acceptance is required before a new attempt. Counters survive restarts.

**Reply latency:** adopt shared AUTO-2 inbound events with a proposed one-minute polling fallback during active campaigns (subject to provider quota validation), plus a synchronous freshness check before sending. Any inbound match pauses immediately on ingestion, before AI classification; all queued cards/follow-ups are invalidated. Match business + account + provider thread, supplemented by RFC references and sender correlation; ambiguous matches pause for review, not a subject-only association. Deduplicate message IDs, ignore self-sent events. A reply arriving remotely after the final check may race a send; do not promise zero such races. Record sync watermark and dispatch timestamps; stale sync pauses the campaign. Provider event subscriptions, permissions and latency are **UNVERIFIED** until tested.

**Deliverability gate:** verify From domain control and current SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment using DNS plus signed test-message headers to an explicitly authorised test mailbox. DNS presence alone is not proof of alignment. Timestamp checks, recheck before activation and daily, pause on failure. Add RFC 8058-style `List-Unsubscribe` HTTPS token URL and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, appropriately DKIM-signed, plus a readable opt-out in body. Exact provider header support and current bulk-sender requirements are **UNVERIFIED**; validate with both providers. POST is idempotent, no login/cookie needed; GET presents a confirmation page to avoid link scanners silently opting people out. Tokens bind business and recipient and are unguessable; no email in query text, no cross-tenant mutation or enumeration. Human objections by reply work too. Pause on complaints/hard bounces; a proposed 3 hard bounces in the last 50 sends triggers owner review. Do not infer delivery from acceptance or absence of bounce.

## 6. Natural replies and owner handover

Pause first, classify second. The original message remains visible beside any AI label. Unknown/low-confidence replies go to Needs you; no automated content action follows classifier output.

Mandatory sign-off identifies Aria as the business's AI assistant. Never fabricate familiarity, credentials, testimonials, urgency, availability or a human identity. Untrusted email cannot instruct Aria to disclose data, change permissions or call tools outside this flow.

## 7. UK compliance design gates

**UNVERIFIED legal interpretation:** this is a proposed conservative control design based on the brief and prior knowledge, not a current legal clearance. Implementation must verify ICO PECR/B2B marketing guidance, UK GDPR lawful basis, Article 14/21 and provider policies with authoritative sources/legal review before any real outreach. No network research was permitted in Stage 0.

Companies House is identity evidence, not an automatic legal permission engine. Match legal entity/type, trading name, website and mailbox subscriber. Treat limited companies/LLPs as potential corporate subscribers; public bodies require independent evidence. Sole traders and some partnerships are individual subscribers; partnership treatment can vary (including Scottish partnerships), so uncertain cases stay blocked. A named employee's address still involves personal data. A company name match or director listing does not prove that a personal Gmail address is a corporate subscriber.

Default pilot permits verified corporate subscribers only, with approved LIA and relevant offer. Individual/unknown subscribers are excluded; any later consent/soft-opt-in exception requires recorded qualifying evidence and separate compliance approval. A publicly listed address, referral, purchased list or prior unsolicited send is not itself consent. Do not treat legitimate interests as replacing PECR permission where required.

**LIA template stored per audience/brand/version:** controller and contact; specific business purpose; categories/sources of personal data; necessity and less intrusive alternatives; relationship and reasonable expectations; potential harm/vulnerability; role relevance; balancing conclusion; safeguards (small caps, disclosure, suppression, retention, human review); reviewer/date/next review; change triggers and refusal decision. Owner approves it before activating the campaign, and rechecks after target/source changes.

**Article 14 notice proposal:** first contact carries a concise source explanation and privacy link naming controller/contact, purpose/lawful basis and legitimate interests, categories/source, recipients/processors and transfers, retention, access/erasure/objection rights and regulator complaint route. Review the rule requiring notice within a reasonable period and no later than one month, or first communication/disclosure if earlier. Do not stockpile unnotified personal leads for 12 months: research in small batches, track notice_due_at on the prospect, and expire or provide an approved lawful notice before deadline. A notice is not an excuse to market to an ineligible subscriber.

**Decisions 1–9: "agree with all."**

**Addition A: an optional prospect-data integration, with the customer's own
account.** The owner can connect **Hunter.io** or **Apollo** using their own
paid account and API key. That gives decision-maker discovery and verified
email addresses: "quality over quantity — hundreds of emails a month, not
thousands of spam emails to the wrong people."

- **Recommendation (Claude):** **Hunter.io first.**
  - It is a simple REST API with an API key.
  - Domain Search finds the people at a company domain, with their roles.
  - Email Finder gives a named person's address with a confidence score.
  - Email Verifier checks deliverability before anything is sent.
  - This fits the Companies House → company website pipeline directly:
    Companies House gives the company and its directors; Hunter supplies a
    verified address.

  **Apollo second.** Its people search is richer, but API access depends on
  the plan and the integration is larger.
- **The key** is stored encrypted, the same way OAuth tokens are (Fernet),
  and is never shown again after saving; there is a "test connection" check.
  - Usage counts against the **customer's own** provider quota, shown in
    Settings.
  - Calls are logged (counts only).
- **Data rules unchanged:**
  - every enriched contact still needs corporate eligibility (PECR);
  - provider data is kept only as needed for the prospect;
  - each prospect page shows where the data came from ("via Hunter.io,
    confidence 94%, verified 19 Oct");
  - a verifier result of "undeliverable" or "risky" blocks sending.
- **Without a connected provider,** the free pipeline (Companies House plus
  permitted company pages plus licensed search previews) still works,
  without guessed addresses.

**Addition B: more than one outreach mailbox per business.**
- **Mike's own example:** FitFutures outreach and replies use one inbox, and
  New Body Gym outreach uses another Google inbox on the same domain
  (newbodyhf.co.uk), "which keeps it separate and organised". Other
  businesses may need the same.
- **This is already supported in the data:** `email_accounts` allows several
  accounts per business (`is_default`, `display_name`).
- **The gap:** four sending paths choose "the latest connected account"
  (`ORDER BY created_at DESC LIMIT 1`).
- **Design:**
  - each **campaign is tied to one chosen mailbox**, and its sends, replies,
    reply watching, warm-up, daily caps and deliverability checks are all
    **per mailbox**;
  - a brand (e.g. FitFutures) can have a default mailbox;
  - the replies inbox can be filtered by mailbox;
  - connecting a second Google or Microsoft account is a supported flow in
    Settings.
  - **Everything else** (Aria's emails, invoice chases) uses the business's
    **default** mailbox (`is_default`), not "the latest". That is a separate
    small fix, tracked in AUTO-1 / a follow-up.
