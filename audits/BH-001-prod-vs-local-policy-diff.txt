WHAT THIS LICENSES
  BH-003's RLS suite runs against the local replay. Because the replay's
  policy TEXT and RLS state match production's, a green run there supports a
  statement about production's POLICY TEXT, not merely about the migration
  files — the gap test_tenant_isolation_rls_path.py's own docstring and
  UNCOVERED registry flagged, and the reason this comparison was the first
  thing done with the CSVs.

  TEXT EQUIVALENCE IS NOT BEHAVIOURAL EQUIVALENCE, and the counterexample is
  in this repository: 030a changed what membership MEANS — it narrowed the
  business_members UPDATE grant and rewrote is_business_member() — without
  changing a single policy expression. Two databases can therefore match on
  all 87 policies and behave differently. Every membership policy here is a
  call into is_business_member() or is_platform_admin(), and this comparison
  says nothing about those functions' bodies, ownership, security settings or
  execute privileges.

WHAT IT DOES NOT LICENSE
  * Grants beyond `businesses` — the Q3 export is truncated at 100 rows.
  * Column-level grants — the Q4 export is truncated and never reached
    `businesses`. This is what RC1 P0-3 turns on.
  * Constraints — no export covers pg_constraint.
  * VIEWS — a view cannot carry RLS and Q1 filters relkind='r'. Production
    has two (receptionist_call_stats, support_stats) that the replay does not
    create; see BH-001-FINDINGS.md §5. The replay is NOT equivalent there.
  * The `auth` and `storage` schemas.
  * Helper FUNCTION bodies, ownership and security settings — the limit that
    matters most, per the paragraph above.
  * Role attributes and role membership.
  * Object ownership of anything: Q1 does not export relowner.
